# Startups (Oink Games, 2017): rules, digital versions, IP

Method note: direct fetches of BoardGameGeek, oinkgames.com, Steam and most review sites were blocked during research, so details come from search-engine extracts of those pages. Everything is cited; items that could not be corroborated are flagged. Verify against the physical rulebook before implementing the rules engine.

## 1. Rules

**Basics.** Designer Jun Sasaki, Oink Games, 2017. 3-7 players, ~20 min, ages 10+ ([Oink](https://oinkgames.com/en/games/analog/startups/), [BGG](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/223770/startups)). It is a re-design of Sasaki's 2015 game *Rights*; Startups added the market, the coin-seeding economy and the Anti-Monopoly chip ([Oink](https://oinkgames.com/en/games/analog/startups/), [Opinionated Gamers](https://opinionatedgamers.com/2017/07/06/startups-review-by-jeff-lingwall/)).

**Components.** 45 company cards in 6 companies, 70 capital chips (two-sided: 1 / 3), 6 Anti-Monopoly chips, 12 point chips (only for the multi-round variant), manual ([retailer listing](https://hamptonhobbiesandgames.co.uk/products/pre-order-startups)). Share counts per company run 5 to 10: Giraffe Beer 5, then 6, 7, 8, 9, Elephant Mars Travel 10 (5+6+7+8+9+10 = 45). Companies: Giraffe Beer, Bowwow Games, Flamingo Soft, Octo Coffee, Hippo Powertech, Elephant Mars Travel ([Opinionated Gamers](https://opinionatedgamers.com/2017/07/06/startups-review-by-jeff-lingwall/), [Oink coaster page](https://oinkgames.com/en/goods/coaster-startups/)). Not confirmed: which of the middle companies has 6/7/8/9.

**Setup.** Shuffle; deal 3 cards to each player; remove 5 cards from the game unseen; each player gets 10 capital chips (1-side up); the rest of the deck is the draw pile; market area starts empty ([whatsericplaying](https://whatsericplaying.com/2018/01/01/startups/), [press.exe](https://press.invincible.ink/game-pile-startups/)).

**Turn = take one card, then play one card** (hand always returns to 3):

1. *Take* either (a) the top card of the draw pile, but first place 1 capital chip on **every** card currently in the market, except cards of a company whose Anti-Monopoly chip you hold; or (b) a face-up card from the market, taking any chips sitting on it. You may **not** take a market card of a company whose Anti-Monopoly chip you hold. On the very first turn only the deck is available ([whatsericplaying](https://whatsericplaying.com/2018/01/01/startups/), [ustudio rules summary](https://ustudio.shop/products/start-up-card-game-oink-games)).
2. *Play* one of your 4 cards either face-up in front of you (your investment/tableau) or face-up into the market. You may not put into the market a card of the same company as the card you just took ([ustudio](https://ustudio.shop/products/start-up-card-game-oink-games), [BGG blog](https://boardgamegeek.com/blog/12180/blogpost/147375/the-startup-problem-featuring-startups)).

**Anti-Monopoly chip.** Whoever has the most face-up cards of a company holds that company's chip. It moves only when someone has strictly more. While holding it you may still play that company from hand, but cannot take that company from the market; in exchange you don't pay chips onto that company's market cards when drawing blind ([whatsericplaying](https://whatsericplaying.com/2018/01/01/startups/), [Dao of Board Gaming](https://daoofboardgaming.home.blog/2025/09/17/startups/)). Tie handling beyond "strictly more takes it" was not spelled out in sources; check the [BGG rules-clarification thread](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/1799753/rules-clarifications).

**Game end and scoring.** The game ends after the player who draws the last deck card finishes their turn. Everyone then adds their 3 hand cards face-up to their tableau (unwanted shares count too). For each company, the sole majority holder is paid by every other holder of that company, 1 chip per share that payer holds; chips received are flipped to the 3 side. Tie for majority: nobody is paid for that company. Most chip value wins ([whatsericplaying](https://whatsericplaying.com/2018/01/01/startups/), [ustudio](https://ustudio.shop/products/start-up-card-game-oink-games), [Opinionated Gamers](https://opinionatedgamers.com/2017/07/06/startups-review-by-jeff-lingwall/)).

**Variants.** Base game is one round; an optional multi-round variant uses the point chips, and reviewers call it the weaker way to play ([Puzzlewick](https://puzzlewick.com/cards/startups/)). There is no official 2-player rule (Oink lists 3-7). A BGG user variant uses a dummy third player dealt 3 cards and 10 chips who plays last ([BGG thread](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2103197/two-player-variant)). A known exploit discussion (cycling cards between hand and market) is at [Major Flaw in Startups?](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2483379/major-flaw-in-startups); the digital rules engine should be tested against it.

## 2. Existing digital versions

- **Official:** *Let's Play! Oink Games* (Oink Games Inc.; Switch Jul 2022; Steam; iOS/Android Dec 2022; Apple Arcade; PS5 Sep 2024; cross-play). Startups is in the base 7-game bundle ($21.99). Startups online supports 1-4 players with CPU fill-in; on Switch it is online/own-screen only because hands are hidden ([Oink news](https://oinkgames.com/en/news/67NMcfHose2eAyGvKQikR4/), [Oink app page](https://oinkgames.com/en/games/digital/lets-play-oink-games/), [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1933490/Lets_Play_Oink_Games/), [NWR review](http://www.nintendoworldreport.com/review/60105/lets-play-oink-games-switch-review)). Ratings: Steam "Mostly/Very Positive" (~78-82% of ~65 reviews); App Store 4.8/5 ([listing](https://mwm.ai/apps/let-s-play-oink-games/1564193305)); reviewers praise value and design, criticise thin solo mode ([Siliconera](https://www.siliconera.com/review-lets-play-oink-games-does-justice-to-japanese-tabletop-gems/)). Notably it caps online play at 4 players, versus 7 in the physical game, and has no random matchmaking or custom cosmetics: this is the gap a new app can fill.
- **Tabletop Simulator:** unofficial fully-scripted Steam Workshop mod with CPU players ([Workshop](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=2225863504)).
- **Board Game Arena / Tabletopia / Yucata:** no implementation found.
- **Open-source clones:** none found on GitHub.

## 3. Intellectual property considerations

- Oink self-publishes its digital adaptations; no public statement on third-party licensing or fan apps was found. Oink Games Inc. holds registered trademarks ([Trademarkia](https://www.trademarkia.com/owners/oink-games-inc)); its Terms of Use assert copyright/trademark over site content ([Oink ToU](https://oinkgames.com/en/page/terms-of-use/)).
- General law: US Copyright Office: "Copyright does not protect the idea for a game, its name or title, or the method or methods for playing it" ([copyright.gov](https://www.copyright.gov/register/tx-games.html)); artwork and rule text are protected; names are trademark territory ([Copyright Alliance](https://copyrightalliance.org/copyrightability-of-board-games/), [Meeple Mountain](https://www.meeplemountain.com/articles/the-board-game-designers-guide-to-intellectual-property-law/)). *Tetris v. Xio* (D.N.J. 2012) held mechanics unprotected but look-and-feel expression protected ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetris_Holding,_LLC_v._Xio_Interactive,_Inc.)).
- **For an unlicensed app, re-theme:** the title "Startups", the six company names and animal mascots/logos, Oink's card art, colour scheme and box trade dress, the Anti-Monopoly chip artwork, and any rulebook prose. The 6-company / 5-10 share distribution, 10-chip start, coin-seeding, chip flip and majority-payout rules are mechanics and may be reimplemented in your own words.
- **Alternative:** approach Oink Games for a licence. They have shown willingness to put their catalogue on digital platforms, but they compete with their own app, so expect a no or a revenue share. Decide this before art production starts.

## 4. Why the game works

- BGG: 7.2 average, overall rank ~891, family rank ~221, weight 1.58/5 ([BGG](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/223770/startups)); ~20 min. Listed among Oink's biggest hits with Scout and Deep Sea Adventure ([BGG thread](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/3577116/impressions-of-startups-a-meaty-filler-from-oink-g)).
- Tension points reviewers cite: every draw from the deck taxes you by subsidising rivals, so "almost every turn is a small, mean decision"; the hidden 3-card hand means the final reveal can flip majorities; the chip flip (1 to 3) makes each majority swing large; the Anti-Monopoly chip creates a leader-brake and a two-player "staredown" for a company ([Puzzlewick](https://puzzlewick.com/cards/startups/), [Tabletop Gaming](https://www.tabletopgaming.co.uk/reviews/startups-review/), [Big Red Barrel](https://www.bigredbarrel.com/2018/11/05/review-startups/)).
- Praise: "tightest little tension engine Oink has packed into a matchbox"; "comes on like a filler but demands so many replays it may fill the evening"; instantly readable colours; a three-act structure. Criticism: first play is confusing, it clicks on the second ([BGG blog](https://boardgamegeek.com/blog/12180/blogpost/147375/the-startup-problem-featuring-startups)); the multi-round variant is weaker. The "confusing first play" point is the single most important onboarding fact for the app.
